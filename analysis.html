Public opinion is often shaped by individual political beliefs, and studying how ideological identity relates to approval of leaders offers insights into political behavior. Using data from the 2020 Cooperative Election Study, which surveyed over 50,000 Americans nationwide, we ask how political ideology predicts presidential approval.

A potential weakness in our model is that unmeasured factors, such as prior exposure to political messaging, may influence both ideology and presidential approval, threatening the assumption of unconfoundedness.
